# Product thesis: Precedent — the honest base-rate engine

**Written:** 2026-11-07, after a market and feasibility research pass (seven research agents; sources at the end).
**For:** Bobby, deciding what to build from the predate work so that it can be marketed, adopted, and pay for itself.
**Status:** A thesis, not a plan. Everything marked *inference* is mine; everything with a source is from the research.

## 1. The one-paragraph version

Build **one card** and put it everywhere: *"The last N times this happened, here is what happened next — n, spread, and a refusal when N is too small."* Make it work for three kinds of "what happened next": what ETFs did, how similar prediction-market questions resolved, and what the next event in the world was. Ship the engine as an open-source, local-first repo with an MCP server (the audience), publish a daily brief generated by the overnight Dream loop (the money), and sell the aligned prediction-market history as an API later (the B2B tail). Drop the causal cockpit as a product; keep it as research. Lead with prediction markets and geopolitics, not stock-picking, because the data is free and unlicensed, the regulatory exposure is lower, the tooling gap is documented, and it is the content your channel already makes.

## 2. What the research actually found

**Nobody sells this to individuals.** The closest analog is Toggle AI, now Reflexivity: its Scenario Analysis is *unavailable* in the Basic tier and "Contact Sales" above it; the top reported complaint is price for individuals. Kensho is enterprise-only through S&P. No retail product was found that maps a current event to historical outcomes with a public price and visible methodology. No open-source repo was found that does event-analog retrieval or overnight contradiction detection (absence of evidence from a limited search, but consistent across three independent passes).

**Forecasters want base rates and have no tool.** A LessWrong project proposed a hand-built base-rate database in 2022; a commenter's worry was selection bias from only-reported events — which is exactly what a strictly-earlier, time-guarded, sample-size-shown design answers. Polymarket and Kalshi expose free price-history and resolution APIs, but cross-venue alignment and entity resolution are left to the user; Dune sells the unified dataset as an enterprise add-on only. Substack and Polymarket expanded their partnership in February 2026, with live odds embeddable in Notes; about one in five of the top 250 paid Substacks already use the features.

**What people pay for, and don't.** Quiver Quantitative: over $2M ARR, about 75% from $30/mo subscriptions and 25% from B2B API, 5,000+ paying from 750k registered users, profitable in 2024 after five-plus years. Citrini Research: at least about $1.2M/yr from a single $1,200 annual tier. Substack median free-to-paid conversion is 3%. Financial Datasets sells a data API through a hosted MCP server at $200/mo personal and $2,000/mo commercial. Users do *not* pay for: chat-with-filings (Fintool was absorbed by Microsoft in April 2026), raw data feeds without a "so what" (the Unusual Whales complaint pattern), consumer news summarizers (Artifact died in a year). No analog showed paid demand for a causal what-if cockpit.

**What makes repos popular.** TradingAgents (~121k stars) and ai-hedge-fund (~64k) are vivid-concept agent demos; infrastructure repos (OpenBB ~75k, Qlib ~50k) grow slower and become companies. No evidence was found that MCP directory listing drives adoption on its own; it is a convenience, not a growth channel.

**Data and cost.** SEC EDGAR: free, exact acceptance timestamps, 10 req/s. GDELT (classic project): "unlimited and unrestricted use for any academic, commercial, or governmental use," redistribution allowed with citation — but *GDELT Cloud* terms are internal-use-only, so use the raw files. Prediction-market prices and resolutions: free public APIs. **Equity price data is the cost breaker**: Tiingo's $30–50 tiers are "Internal Use Only — you may not display or share the data"; a display/derived licence is negotiated, estimated at hundreds of dollars a month. LLM parsing at 5k documents/day is roughly $70/mo on a Haiku-class API (price unverified) or near-free but machine-hogging on the Mac. Gross margin on a $29/mo product with 500 subscribers is about 92% *if* the price licence stays near $500/mo.

**Regulatory.** US: Lowe v. SEC publisher's exclusion — impersonal, disinterested, regular circulation; a Seeking Alpha class action was dismissed under it in August 2024. Canada: Ontario's "business trigger" analysis is narrower and a publisher's exemption was once revoked for five years in a disciplinary case; get a securities lawyer before charging Canadians for anything that names equities. Prediction markets are CFTC event contracts, not securities — lower exposure, still disclaim.

## 3. The design decision the research forces

The four predate tracks, graded against the evidence:

| Track | Market evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Precedent** (analog engine) | Documented gap, most shareable unit, cheap, three independent briefs converge on it | **The product.** |
| **Dream** (overnight contradiction diff) | Novel, nobody ships it, but "needs the reader's context" to be shareable | **The content engine** behind the brief, not a standalone product. |
| **Ripple** (supply-chain shock map) | Retail gap is real but needs equity price display rights and 10-K extraction at scale | **Phase 3 paid feature**, after the price licence exists. |
| **Mechanism** (causal cockpit) | No paid demand found; LLM-proposed graphs are hard to defend publicly | **Research and content only.** Build it for the channel, not the product. |

And one change to Precedent itself: the response library should hold **three outcome types**, not one.

1. **Asset response** (already designed): abnormal return of SPY, sector ETFs, TLT, GLD, USO after the event. Needs the price licence to *display*; fine to compute locally for the open-source user who brings their own data.
2. **Market-resolution response** (new): for an event, the Polymarket/Kalshi questions that were open at the time and how they resolved. "After the last 9 unexpected central-bank holds, the 'rate cut by next meeting' markets resolved YES 2 of 9 times." Free data, no licence, the forecaster's actual question.
3. **Event-sequence response** (new): what the *next* typed event was. "After 23 coup attempts since 2015 (GDELT), sanctions by a G7 member followed within 30 sessions in 14." Free, commercial-OK, point-in-time, not a security. This is the layer your channel can broadcast.

Each layer uses the same time guard, the same story clustering, the same refusal rule. The card is the same. Only the outcome column changes.

## 4. The three surfaces

### 4.1 The repo: `precedent` (audience)

Open-source, local-first, Apache-2.0. Install, point it at EDGAR + GDELT + the market APIs, and run `precedent "<headline>"`. The README's first screenshot is the card with a visible **REFUSED: 3 precedents, need 8** state — the research's pick for the single most shareable feature, because every competitor shows a confident number and this one visibly declines. Ships with an MCP server so anyone in Claude, ChatGPT or Cursor can ask "what happened the last twelve times a sovereign defaulted?" and get the card with sources.

Why this gets stars (*inference*, grounded in the repo pattern): it has a vivid concept ("the AI that refuses to guess"), a two-minute try-it path, and it rides the prediction-market wave that TradingAgents-style repos have not touched. Launch: Show HN, an X thread with three cards on that week's headlines, and a retro-TV "BASE RATE DESK" segment on Disinfotainment. Your channel's aesthetic is an asset here; the agent-demo repos won on concept, not code.

### 4.2 The brief (money)

A daily or three-times-weekly email generated by Dream + Precedent and edited by you for thirty minutes. Fixed format, so it is a product and not a blog:

- **Base rates for today's three biggest headlines** — the card, with the Polymarket odds embedded beside the historical rate: *"market says 34%, base rate says 52% (n=19, wide)"*. The gap between the two is the entire editorial hook, and it is impersonal by construction.
- **Overnight** — the Dream diff: *N* contradictions between sources, *M* silent shifts, what expired.
- **One mechanism** — occasionally, the causal cockpit for a single event, as a figure. Research content, never a recommendation.

Free list from day one; paid tier at about $15–20/mo once the free list passes ~1,000 (archive, full precedent tables, the MCP key). At Substack's 3% median: 5k free → ~150 paid → ~$27k/yr; 20k free → ~$108k/yr. Citrini and Quiver show the ceiling is far higher with an audience and an API tail. Platform: Substack, because of the native Polymarket embeds and Notes distribution.

### 4.3 The API (B2B tail, phase 3)

The aligned, entity-resolved, point-in-time history of prediction-market questions joined to typed events is exactly what Dune sells enterprise-only and what the data-vendor post says people build by hand. Sell it the Financial Datasets way: free credits, $200/mo personal, commercial tier with redistribution, all through the same MCP server. Quiver's 25% B2B share is the template. Equity-side responses join this tier once a price licence is negotiated; until then the API returns ETF responses as *your derived statistics only*, which a vendor must confirm in writing.

## 5. Why this beats the alternatives considered

- **A prosumer SaaS web app first** (Quiver's path): proven at $30/mo, but took five years and 750k free users, carries the most regulatory exposure (a what-if cockpit that takes a user's portfolio is personalization), and needs the price licence on day one. Later, not first.
- **Selling the event dataset first**: only clean for EDGAR/GDELT/press-release-derived events; no distribution without the audience; Quiver's B2B share came *after* the consumer product.
- **A non-finance pivot (competitive intelligence)**: real budgets (Klue and Crayon medians about $30k/yr), but enterprise sales cycles and it throws away everything the channel can amplify. Keep as a fallback if the free list is under 500 at day 90.
- **Equities-first positioning**: every cost and legal problem in the research (price display licence, Ontario business trigger, "is this advice") attaches to equities. Prediction markets and world events have none of them and a documented tooling gap.

## 6. What changes in the predate plans

1. **Spine Task 16 (GDELT) moves up** to sit beside Task 5 (EDGAR). GDELT is the free, commercial-OK, point-in-time event stream the product runs on. Use the raw 15-minute files, not GDELT Cloud, and cite GDELT in every output.
2. **Precedent Task 2 gets two sibling response libraries**: `build_market_resolution_library` (Polymarket CLOB + Gamma, Kalshi historical; question text → typed event key via the same parser; outcome = resolution) and `build_event_sequence_library` (outcome = first typed event of each type within *h* sessions). Same `find_precedents`, same `summarize_responses`, same refusal.
3. **Precedent Task 8's card** gains the `n` / spread / REFUSED layout as the canonical output, and a `--card PNG` flag, because the screenshot *is* the distribution channel.
4. **Dream Tasks 1–6 become the content engine**; the brief writer is Dream Task 5's Markdown with a template and the day's three Precedent cards.
5. **Mechanism** stays as written but is explicitly research; its cockpit feeds the brief's "one mechanism" slot and the channel, not a paid feature.
6. **Ripple** is unchanged but gated on a price licence.
7. **The MCP server** is a new small task after Precedent Task 8: `precedent-mcp` exposing `precedents(headline|key, horizon, outcome_type)` and `card(...)`, local by default, remote with OAuth later.

## 7. Risks, stated plainly

- **GDELT coding is noisy.** Its CAMEO event types are famously over-triggered. The parser's re-typing of the linked article is the fix, and it is also the cost. Measure parser precision on GDELT-linked articles before trusting event-sequence base rates.
- **Analog matching for prediction-market questions is the hard part.** Question text is heterogeneous; the closed event schema is what makes it tractable, and some question types will have no analogs. Refuse loudly; that is the brand.
- **Selection bias is real.** Only reported events enter the library. Show it: the card should say what the library covers (sources, years, event types) under the number.
- **3% conversion is the median, not a floor.** With no finance audience, 5k free subscribers is many months of consistent publishing. The channel helps; it does not guarantee it.
- **Canada.** Get an hour with a securities lawyer before charging Canadians for anything that names a security. Serving US readers through a US-hosted Substack and keeping the equity layer "what followed, not what to do" is the lower-exposure route.
- **The frozen-model narrative cuts both ways.** "ChatGPT's knowledge is frozen; this wakes up every morning having re-read the world" is a strong pitch (and literally the problem Google's sleep framework attacks at the weight level). But the research pipeline must *never* fine-tune on event content, or every backtest is contaminated (spec 4.4). Weight-level learning, if ever, is on parsing quality only.
